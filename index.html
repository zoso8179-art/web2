The Problem

Some months your revenue is strong and some months it's thin, and nobody can quite say why. You can't tell whether next quarter's number is safe, so every decision to hire, spend or grow feels like a gamble. Or the business is growing so fast that you're starting to wonder whether the way you sell can carry the weight.

Common reasons behind unpredictable revenue

Which of your deals are definite, which are possible, and which are a wishcast?

When quarter end comes around, the number on the board usually depends on who had a good week.

Nobody actually knows how much pipeline is enough.

You hold £2M against a £1M target and assume you're safe, but without your real win rates, you're guessing.

No decision is the competitor nobody tracks.

Buyers get cold feet and decide that doing nothing is safer.

Your deals rely on one person at the customer.

If your contact leaves or gets overruled, months of work vanish with them.

The relationships you've invested so much time and money in sit with one person.

When revenue lives in one person's head and diary, momentum stops the second they get busy, or worse, leave.

About Me

I've spent over 20 years in B2B sales. I've carried a number myself, led sales teams, and run a regional business.

That means I've been on both sides of the forecast call. I've been the person giving or committing to the number, and also the person relying on that number being solid.

What I've seen is that unpredictable sales is rarely down to people not working hard. The factors vary business by business, but the same patterns show up in every organisation I speak to, whether it's people meaning different things by a qualified deal, or nobody knowing how much pipeline is enough.

Talented people, a positive culture and strong customer relationships are incredibly valuable. But they need to sit on a repeatable, predictable sales process that everyone in the business understands. I'd like to understand how that works in your business, and help you build it.
