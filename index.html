The Founder

The framework was built on the floor, not in a lecture theatre.

His experience spans enterprise sales, go-to-market strategy, commercial transformation, revenue operations and sales leadership — across government, retail, financial services and the public sector. He has led organisations through market change, shifted businesses toward recurring revenue models, and delivered above target in some of the most competitive, regulated markets in the UK. He has also served as a Non-Executive Go-to-Market Director, advising boards on commercial strategy and forecasting from outside the operator seat.

Nick has carried a personal target, and he's owned an £80m+ P&L. Not many people running commercial diagnostics have stood on both sides of that line, and it changes what you see. As a rep, he knew every trick that gets a deal over the line by the end of the quarter. Running the P&L, he watched those same tricks from the other side — and realised most of them weren't hacks at all. They were symptoms of a system that hadn't been built properly, so people were improvising around gaps that shouldn't have existed.

That's not a knock on the sales team. Reps do what the system rewards. Build the system properly, and the same people start compounding instead of firefighting. That's the mechanism behind FILTER, and it repeats:

  • Account-based experience (ABX). Aligned sales, marketing and customer success around the same target accounts, and fixed CRM hygiene first so the cadence built on top of it could be trusted. Not more leads — just meticulous enough to stop losing deals that should have been won.
  • Coverage through acquisition. Resized and restructured sales coverage pre and post-deal, without losing revenue in the handover.
  • Channel without cannibalising direct. Built both motions to grow in parallel, not at each other's expense.
  • Portfolio rationalisation. Cut an overextended product line down to what the market and the sales resource could actually support — working across product, sales engineering and marketing to make it stick.

ARC is how that experience becomes something other people can use. Not just a revenue architecture system — a way to win, predictably, at scale.
